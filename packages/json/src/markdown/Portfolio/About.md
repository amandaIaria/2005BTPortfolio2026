### V1
I've gone from a LAMP stack developer to someone who focuses on UX/UI with growing brand awareness. I've worked on small to large codebases, from legacy to new codebases and from small state sized to large global company. 

<i>Alex can identify and resolve issues with persistence and dedication and is an exceptional communicator excited about learning software while collaborating with others. They can be resourceful, reliable, and professional. Alex is known for their strong work ethic and working well in the fast-paced environment. Some of they're skills include ES6 Javascript, React, Next.js and Tanstack, S/CSS, and Sitecore.</i>

So, what does all that mean? Before I was doing this professionally, I dug into it as a hobby working on fansites for some of my favorite shows. That was a big thing in the mid 2000s, people would create them and just have fun. This was the era were there was personality in a website, even if it was a bad design. 

In 2010 I decided to finish my degree and go into web development. If you're stuck in life why not do something you enjoy and so this was the start of getting into my career. In 2013 I graduated from Champlain College and started my first career job at HMC advertising a month later.

At HMC I delved into the what it means to work with clients. Learning how data and design can influence people and where dark design patterns can manipulate them was an eye opener, and shaped how I understud marketing practises. The sites I developed, Snap, Parent Up, My Healthy VT, Good Food Truck, 802Quits were the opposite of those ideas. They were ment to help people, not maniplulate them into buying something. It was great to find out that the data said that they were. More people we visiting the new or updated sites then their previous versions.

In 2016 I joined Dealer.com, one of the largest (at least at the time) Tech company in Vermont and worked with a team that was assigned to Suberu New England. This was the first time I worked in a product company. This is when ideas like CI/CD were starting up and React was starting to take hold. I found that componetizing was where we should go. Create something that could be customized and used in different places instead of relying on pages.

However, in just a year and a half I would be laid off. This is the longest year I had without work, but I was still learning. I delved into Unity with C# since I had the backbone of the language from college. I didn't make anything great or something I would sell. Instead I learned how interactivty can change your perception on, lack of a better phrase, of the thing you are inter acting with. Something that feels like you can hold can make something not boring.

At the end of this year I moved to Boston and started work with a UX team at Mapfre. This was the largest corporation I've worked for, and it had it's own brand of challenges but the ideas I had learned before was in use here. I've always liked how we could decontruct the most basic of elements (both in science and in development). How does the interaction of a basic form mean someone will or will not fill it? How does the same form's layout do the same thing? Here I delved more into accessibility and seeing how we could help someone complete what they need to do. But that's not just it, I also found how Design Systems were the future.

I don't know if you remember  but the 2010's design styles were going into a direction that yes made things more useable but the personality is gone. A lot of sites where using Bootstrap, MaterialUI,  and Foundation. CSS3 gave us so many tools to accomplish great design, but we were falling back to these same design systems. It's still a problem in my opinion, but design systems allow the brand to be in charge. So, that's how Breakpoint was born. A system that brought together componets, brand, voice, and iconagraphy. It was a dream to share it with the non-US based companies within Mapfre, but that never happened.

Around 2020 I felt that I needed to move on and get back into a small company were I felt I had a voice. I joined Thankful, which by now has been gobbled up by Gladly, was and will the only time I will work with a startup. What did I learn here? Not much, other than not working in a framework because the CTO doesn't understand them is not a good excuse.

Finally we've reached the present, and I may have found the type of work I should do. Velir is a fully remote digital agency. I have really loved doing different kinds of projects with different kinds of people. It allows you to learn more and not stay to stagnate on what you can do. Plus it is a bit easier to share things I'm actually glad to work on. Accessibility, React, and the multiple CMS we've worked has shown us the multitude of clients that need help.

But what am I looking for in the future? I'm not sure. I would love to create more art, more interactions, and even see the weird internet come back. Places like NeoCities bring back the weird and fun. Maybe I'll create something that brings back that.
