## Body

In 1994 the movie Stargate came out. I was a kid who was still obsessed with anything scifi and Egypt. What a perfect storm! Then in 97 it was announced that a TV show would air, and boi it did. Set as a sequal to the movie a new team is started to retrive Daniel, who stayed behind after the original mission. 

This was a show that I grew up with and still watch to this day. Even though the Egypt and Ancient world inspirations were soon pushed away for the Ancients, it was still a show I devoured.

### Apophis Arc

Main article: [Apophis (_Stargate_)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apophis_\(Stargate\) "Apophis (Stargate)")

The pilot episode ("[Children of the Gods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children_of_the_Gods "Children of the Gods")"), set one year after the events of the original feature film, introduces the Goa'uld [System Lord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/System_Lord "System Lord") [Apophis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apophis_\(Stargate\) "Apophis (Stargate)") ([Peter Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Williams_\(actor\) "Peter Williams (actor)")) as the main antagonist when he attacks Earth's mothballed SGC military base through the Stargate and kidnaps an airman. The SGC is brought back into action when the Stargate is revealed to be part of an interplanetary network connecting countless planets. SG teams are created to help defend Earth against the Goa'uld, who have interstellar pyramid warships and vast armies of [Jaffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa_\(Stargate\) "Jaffa (Stargate)") (hereditary slaves and human incubators to the Goa'uld) at their disposal. Earth's flagship team SG-1, which includes Apophis's defected First Prime (lead Jaffa soldier) [Teal'c](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teal'c "Teal'c"), initiates several alliances with other cultures in the galaxy, such as the Goa'uld-like but truly symbiotic [Tok'ra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tok'ra "Tok'ra"), the advanced human [Tollan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_recurring_alien_characters_in_Stargate_SG-1#Tollan "List of recurring alien characters in Stargate SG-1"), the pacifist [Nox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nox_\(Stargate\) "Nox (Stargate)"), the benevolent [Roswell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roswell_Incident "Roswell Incident")-alien [Asgard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asgard_\(Stargate\) "Asgard (Stargate)") and remnants of the powerful Ancients. Another alien threat arises in the [Season 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargate_SG-1_\(season_3\) "Stargate SG-1 (season 3)") finale ("[Nemesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemesis_\(Stargate_SG-1\) "Nemesis (Stargate SG-1)")") in the form of sentient machines called [Replicators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Replicator_\(Stargate\) "Replicator (Stargate)"). Meanwhile, rogue agents of a shadowy intelligence agency on Earth, the [NID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NID_\(Stargate\) "NID (Stargate)"), repeatedly attempt to take control of the Stargate and other alien technology. Despite Apophis's death in the [Season 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargate_SG-1_\(season_5\) "Stargate SG-1 (season 5)") premiere ("[Enemies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enemies_\(Stargate_SG-1\) "Enemies (Stargate SG-1)")"), the [Goa'uld Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goa'uld_Empire "Goa'uld Empire") remains a major foe in _Stargate SG-1_ until the end of [Season 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargate_SG-1_\(season_8\) "Stargate SG-1 (season 8)").

### Anubis Arc

Main article: [Anubis (_Stargate_)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anubis_\(Stargate\) "Anubis (Stargate)")

After Apophis's defeat, the half-Ascended Goa'uld System Lord [Anubis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anubis_\(Stargate\) "Anubis (Stargate)") ([David Palffy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Palffy "David Palffy")) assumes the role of the primary antagonist of the show. This new villain possesses much of the knowledge of the Ancients and their technology. While Earth builds its first interstellar spaceship (the _[Prometheus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prometheus_\(Stargate\) "Prometheus (Stargate)")_) in seasons [Season 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargate_SG-1_\(season_6\) "Stargate SG-1 (season 6)") and [Season 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargate_SG-1_\(season_7\) "Stargate SG-1 (season 7)"), Anubis creates an army of almost invincible [Kull Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kull_Warrior "Kull Warrior") and wipes out or subordinates most of his adversaries amongst the System Lords. In the Season 7 finale ("[Lost City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_City_\(Stargate_SG-1\) "Lost City (Stargate SG-1)")"), SG-1 discovers a powerful weapon in an Ancient outpost in Antarctica that annihilates Anubis's entire fleet and also sets the stage for the spin-off series _[Stargate Atlantis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargate_Atlantis "Stargate Atlantis")_. Ba'al subsumes much of Anubis's power in [Season 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargate_SG-1_\(season_8\) "Stargate SG-1 (season 8)"), while Anubis, who survived the destruction of his fleet in a disembodied form, quietly begins to re-assert his influence. Human-form Replicators begin to conquer the System Lords, but SG-1 finds and adjusts an Ancient weapon to destroy all Replicators throughout the galaxy. Near the end of Season 8 ("[Threads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threads_\(Stargate_SG-1\) "Threads (Stargate SG-1)")"), it is revealed that the benevolent Ascended being [Oma Desala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oma_Desala "Oma Desala") ([Mel Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mel_Harris "Mel Harris")) is responsible for Anubis's original ascension. When she engages Anubis in an eternal stalemated battle on the Ascended plane to prevent his acting on the mortal plane, the Replicators and most System Lords have already been annihilated and the Jaffa win their freedom from Goa'uld rule.

### Ori Arc

Main article: [Ori (_Stargate_)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ori_\(Stargate\) "Ori (Stargate)")

The original SG-1 team disbands after the events of Season 8 but slowly reunites under new team leader [Lt Col. Cameron Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_Mitchell_\(Stargate\) "Cameron Mitchell (Stargate)") after the SGC inadvertently draws the attention of the Ori to the existence of sentient life in the Milky Way; the Ori are revealed to be a faction of ascended Ancients residing in another galaxy that are diametrically opposed to the Ancients' belief in strict noninterference in the lower planes of existence, sapping the energy from untold billions of "lower beings" (non-ascended sentient beings) by means of their worship in a religion called Origin. While the Ori send enhanced human beings named [Priors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prior_\(Stargate\) "Prior (Stargate)") to the Milky Way to convert the galaxy to Origin, Ba'al and some minor Goa'uld infiltrate Earth through _The Trust_ (a coalition of rogue NID operatives) to rebuild their power. At the end of [Season 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargate_SG-1_\(season_9\) "Stargate SG-1 (season 9)") ("Camelot (Part 1)"), the Ori begin an evangelistic crusade with their warships and effortlessly wipe out the combined fleet of Earth and its allies. The leader of the Ori, [Adria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adria_\(Stargate\) "Adria (Stargate)") ([Morena Baccarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morena_Baccarin "Morena Baccarin")), is introduced in the premiere of [Season 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargate_SG-1_\(season_10\) "Stargate SG-1 (season 10)") ("Flesh and Blood (Part 2)"). SG-1 searches for the Sangraal, an Ancient weapon that might defeat the Ori, while Ba'al and his clones attempt to find the weapon for their own purposes. With the help of the powerful Ancient [Merlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merlin_\(Stargate\) "Merlin (Stargate)") ([Matthew Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Walker_\(Canadian_actor\) "Matthew Walker (Canadian actor)")), SG-1 finds the construction plans of the Sangraal and sends a working version to the Ori galaxy. Shortly thereafter, Adria ascends. The direct-to-DVD film _[Stargate: The Ark of Truth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargate:_The_Ark_of_Truth "Stargate: The Ark of Truth")_ ends the Ori Arc. The only influential Goa'uld in the last two seasons of _Stargate SG-1_ is the System Lord [Ba'al](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba'al_\(Stargate\) "Ba'al (Stargate)") ([Cliff Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cliff_Simon "Cliff Simon")), who is defeated in the direct-to-DVD film _[Stargate: Continuum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargate:_Continuum "Stargate: Continuum")_.
