## Body

Sanctuary started life as a web series in 2007 before Syfy picked it up as a full show in 2008. It's got that late 2000s green-screen look, almost everything shot on a soundstage with the backgrounds painted in digitally, which gives it this weird, half-real quality that either pulls you in or throws you right out. For me it pulled me in.

At the center of it is Dr. Helen Magnus, a 157 year old scientist who runs a network of sanctuaries around the world, safe houses for "abnormals," basically anything that isn't strictly human. Some of these creatures are monstrous, some are just misunderstood, and the show spends a lot of time in that gray area instead of just doing monster of the week.

Where it gets really good is the Victorian backstory. Magnus was part of a group called the Five, along with Nikola Tesla, John Druitt, James Watson, and Nigel Griffin, all of them Victorian era scientists who experimented on themselves with abnormal source blood and came out the other side with extended life and strange abilities. Half the fun of the show is those flashbacks and how they tie into what's happening in the present day plotlines with the Cabal.

It only ran four seasons before Syfy cancelled it, and the ending feels a little rushed because of that, but I still go back to it.

### Cast

Dr. Helen Magnus: Founder and head of the Sanctuary network. Over 150 years old thanks to abnormal blood, brilliant, and carrying about a century and a half of guilt and secrets around with her.

Will Zimmerman: A criminal psychologist Magnus recruits early on. He's the audience surrogate for a while, the normal guy getting pulled into her world, but he grows into it fast.

Kate Freelander: A former con artist and thief who ends up working security for the Sanctuary. Prickly, funny, good in a fight.

Henry Foss: The resident tech genius who also happens to be a werewolf, or an "HAP" as the show insists on calling it. Builds most of the gadgets and holds the place together.

John Druitt: Also known as Jack the Ripper. Magnus's former lover from her Victorian days, gained the ability to teleport through space after the Five's experiments, and spends most of the show somewhere between villain and tragic ally.

Ashley Magnus: Helen's daughter, a field agent for the Sanctuary. Leaves the show partway through, which changes the whole family dynamic for the back half.

### Seasons

Season one is mostly setup, introducing Will, the concept of abnormals, and the network of sanctuaries, while dropping hints about Magnus's past with the Five.

Season two digs further into the Cabal, a shadowy organization that wants to weaponize abnormals, and it's also where Ashley's arc comes to a head.

Season three shifts things again after the Cabal storyline wraps, bringing John Druitt further into the fold and leaning harder into the Victorian era mythology.

Season four goes in a different direction entirely with the Hollow Earth arc, and it's where you can really feel the show trying to wrap things up before cancellation caught up with it.
